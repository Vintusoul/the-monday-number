The Monday Number

Why product engineers still matter when AI writes the code.

Under two minutes, with narration and sound.

A client asked for more charts.

What they needed, they never said. This is a short case for why product engineers still matter when AI writes the code.

“We need more value from the dashboard.”

That was the whole brief. The client knew something was missing and couldn't name it.

The easy answer was more charts.

AI could have built them in an afternoon. Most would have gone unopened: 80% of features in the average software product are rarely or never used.

Pendo Feature Adoption Report, 2019

Then a support ticket came in.

The system filed it as resolved. I read it as a warning from someone who had stopped expecting a fix.

Support ticket

Third time the export has failed this week. Fine. I'll just do it manually. Thanks anyway.

ResolvedChurn risk

So I asked why. Four times.

Each answer moved further from the dashboard and closer to one person's week.

  1. Why?The reports take too long to get anything out of.
  2. Why?I rebuild the same four numbers every Monday.
  3. Why?The board meets at ten and asks what changed.
  4. Why?I need to walk in already knowing the answer.

One number, before the 10am board meeting.

The dashboard already held the data. The client needed the answer waiting in their inbox on Monday, without rebuilding it by hand.

AI wrote most of the code. The conversation decided what to build.

In this case the Monday spreadsheet was retired and the dashboard stayed as it was.

74% of companies have yet to show tangible value from AI.

The same study puts around 70% of the obstacles down to people and process. That gap closes when someone understands how the business runs.

BCG, Where's the Value in AI?, 2024

66% of developers are frustrated by AI answers that are almost right.

Someone has to know what right looks like. That knowledge comes from the user and the business, and no tool hands it to you.

Stack Overflow Developer Survey, 2025

AI builds what you ask for. A product engineer finds out what to ask.

Understanding people is still the job.

Christelle Zeederberg, product engineer
